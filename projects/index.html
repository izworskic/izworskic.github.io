Where should I go for fall color?

Michigan Fall Color

I built a statewide map and regional planning system that brings color timing, weather context, and named destinations together. It helps readers compare places instead of relying on one statewide peak date.

The model blends regional seasonal timing with available weather and vegetation inputs, accounting for source age. Estimates and AI-written regional summaries are identified as interpretation, with dates and sources to inspect.

When is a ship worth going to watch?

Soo Locks Ship Schedule

I brought vessel positions, a local map, and viewing context into a planning tool for Sault Ste. Marie. Its next-ship engine uses fresh AIS reports to identify approaching freighters and estimate arrival windows along the shipping channel.

The geometry distinguishes upbound and downbound channels around the river’s islands. A planning window remains an estimate; lock operations and incomplete AIS coverage can change what a visitor sees.

Where is the freighter I want to see?

Great Lakes Ship Tracker

I built a lake-wide viewing page with quick choices for the Soo, Mackinac, Duluth, Port Huron, Detroit River, and Saginaw Bay. Vessel reports, corridor views, passage-source links, and nearby NOAA observations give shoreside watchers a useful starting point.

The public AIS providers supply the positions; my contribution is the interface and regional context. A missing vessel does not mean empty water, and nearby weather observations are not measurements aboard a ship.

What does today’s bridge status mean for my trip?

Mackinac Bridge Live

I combined the Bridge Authority’s current status, vehicle-specific guidance, cameras, and approach-weather context in one crossing tool. Drivers can distinguish an open bridge from an advisory, escort requirement, or closure that matters to their vehicle.

The official Bridge Authority report takes priority. Nearby NOAA observations and forecasts are labeled separately so off-bridge weather does not become a claimed deck measurement or an invented restriction.

NYC Crossing Decision Engine

The NYC Crossing Decision Engine separates crossing cost from crossing conditions. Its cost model accounts for payment, vehicle class, destination-zone entry, and applicable credits. Its traffic layer identifies the source and scope of each reading. A fixed crossing probe is presented as a crossing observation rather than a complete address-to-address journey.

The reliability work is inspectable in traffic.js. Routing calls have bounded timeouts and limited retries; failed or partial routing results do not receive the normal successful-result cache lifetime. The regression tests exercise recovery, cache behavior, invalid responses, and source priority. The production smoke script checks the page and API together and reports degraded coverage when a source is unavailable.

Why a failed request must not become a cached answer

A traffic timeout is evidence about a request, not evidence that a bridge is clear or congested. The implementation starts five fixed Mapbox crossing probes concurrently. Each attempt has an eight-second deadline, with at most one retry for a timeout or server error. A known client error is not retried. The routing budget is therefore bounded at sixteen seconds, plus the separate bounded cache read; it is not a promise about total page load time.

Before accepting a route, the code checks its duration and distance against the intended crossing, and rejects supplied waypoints that snap too far from an anchor. A response that cannot be normalized or fails those checks does not become a live observation. Only a complete, usable LIVE probe set receives the normal cache lifetimes: sixty seconds in memory and ninety seconds in Redis. PARTIAL and UNAVAILABLE results remain eligible to recover on the next request. Older cached failure documents are ignored too.

For example, if one of five probes times out while four return usable routes, the result is partial coverage. It must not hide the failed probe behind a successful-result cache. If the next request obtains all five usable routes, it can return live coverage immediately. This is an illustrative failure sequence, not a claim that an outage occurred at a particular time.

What the tests prove, and what production still has to show

The tests use controlled responses and a simulated clock to verify the timeout budget, one-retry limit, recovery, and absence of cache writes for failures. They also verify that official agency observations take priority where available. Passing those tests establishes the software contract; it does not establish that every outside feed is healthy today. The production smoke check separately reads the deployed page and API, validates the returned crossing records, and reports a temporary feed outage as degraded coverage.

Traffic observations also retain their scope. An approach segment, a crossing probe, and an address-to-address trip answer different questions. Mixing them into a “fastest route” ranking would imply a comparison the evidence cannot support. The tool keeps the cost decision useful while showing where current traffic evidence is incomplete.

Niagara Border Crossing Planner

The Niagara Border Crossing Planner compares crossings for a particular direction, traveler, and lane. It incorporates the additional travel involved in a diversion, checks whether observations are fresh enough to support a recommendation, and exposes material disagreements between federal and operator reports.

Its comparison implementation can return insufficient evidence instead of naming a winner. The tests cover stale observations, source conflicts, traveler restrictions, and the public page's integration with the API. This is a concrete example of a product decision encoded in software: a detour recommendation needs better evidence than a lower number in one feed.

Michigan Trout Report

The Michigan Trout Report brings stream flows, water temperatures, and weather context together for Michigan anglers. Named river pages, technique guides, hatch references, and the daily report support planning around a particular river. Chris's founder profile describes his connection to the project.

The report methodology follows an observation through freshness checks, the daily historical flow comparison, software ratings, and the written summary. It explains which parts are measurements and which are interpretation.

Michigan Outdoors Now

Michigan Outdoors Now brings Michigan day-trip planning, trail and access information, current conditions, and travel context into one project. It extends the same interest in place-specific decisions to a day outside.

Michigan Birding Report

The Michigan Birding Report covers Michigan bird sightings, county-level birding context, and notable species pages built around live observation data. The daily birding report publishes a fresh Michigan birding briefing each day. Both sites are designed around the rhythm of a working Michigan birder rather than the more generic checklist style of larger national platforms.

Great Lakes Levels Intelligence

Great Lakes Levels places current water levels in historical and shoreline context. It organizes information by lake and named regions, including Saginaw Bay, for people trying to understand their shoreline. Chris's connection to the bay and his Save Our Shoreline civic work explain the project's focus.

The public repository contains deployed HTML and compiled JavaScript assets, rather than the original React source. Its README explains that boundary and the static deployment workflow. The page verification script checks canonical URLs and consistent creator attribution across the published HTML.

Perfect Lawn Advisor

The Perfect Lawn Advisor at lawn.chrisizworski.com generates a graded lawn plan from a single Michigan address. The tool combines soil, weather, and turfgrass context with a Claude-generated plan, then caches the result so identical addresses return instantly. Cornerstone articles, a city directory, and a curated shop layer support the core planning workflow.

Great Lakes Gazette

The Great Lakes Gazette is a Great Lakes maritime publication connecting vessel movement, lake conditions, and regional weather context. It gives the region's shipping and water information a place in the broader publishing work.

Freighter View Farms

Freighter View Farms is the seed-saving and Great Lakes gardening blog. The site focuses on heirloom vegetables, Zone 6a Michigan gardening, and the slow accumulation of a place-based seed library on the western shore of Saginaw Bay. Writing on the site is intentionally literary and grounded in a specific garden rather than generic gardening advice.
