Chris Izworski Public Projects

What each project does, the decisions behind it, and where to inspect the work.

Chris Izworski builds and maintains public web tools and publishing projects from Bay City, Michigan. These projects use different kinds of evidence: official traffic readings, river gauges, bird observations, historical lake records, and a garden journal. The engineering task is to make that evidence useful without making it appear more certain than it is.

NYC Crossing Decision Engine

The NYC Crossing Decision Engine separates crossing cost from crossing conditions. Its cost model accounts for payment, vehicle class, destination-zone entry, and applicable credits. Its traffic layer identifies the source and scope of each reading. A fixed crossing probe is presented as a crossing observation rather than a complete address-to-address journey.

The reliability work is inspectable in traffic.js. Routing calls have bounded timeouts and limited retries; failed or partial routing results do not receive the normal successful-result cache lifetime. The regression tests exercise recovery, cache behavior, invalid responses, and source priority. The production smoke script checks the page and API together and reports degraded coverage when a source is unavailable.

Niagara Border Crossing Planner

The Niagara Border Crossing Planner compares crossings for a particular direction, traveler, and lane. It incorporates the additional travel involved in a diversion, checks whether observations are fresh enough to support a recommendation, and exposes material disagreements between federal and operator reports.

Its comparison implementation can return insufficient evidence instead of naming a winner. The tests cover stale observations, source conflicts, traveler restrictions, and the public page's integration with the API. This is a concrete example of a product decision encoded in software: a detour recommendation needs better evidence than a lower number in one feed.

Michigan Outdoors Now

Michigan Outdoors Now brings Michigan day-trip planning, trail and access information, current conditions, and travel context into one project. It extends the same interest in place-specific decisions to a day outside.

Michigan Trout Report

The Michigan Trout Report brings stream flows, water temperatures, and weather context together for Michigan anglers. Named river pages, technique guides, hatch references, and the daily report support planning around a particular river. Chris's founder profile describes his connection to the project.

Michigan Birding Report

The Michigan Birding Report covers Michigan bird sightings, county-level birding context, and notable species pages built around live observation data. The daily birding report publishes a fresh Michigan birding briefing each day. Both sites are designed around the rhythm of a working Michigan birder rather than the more generic checklist style of larger national platforms.

Great Lakes Levels Intelligence

Great Lakes Levels places current water levels in historical and shoreline context. It organizes information by lake and named regions, including Saginaw Bay, for people trying to understand their shoreline. Chris's connection to the bay and his Save Our Shoreline civic work explain the project's focus.

The public repository contains deployed HTML and compiled JavaScript assets, rather than the original React source. Its README explains that boundary and the static deployment workflow. The page verification script checks canonical URLs and consistent creator attribution across the published HTML.

Perfect Lawn Advisor

The Perfect Lawn Advisor at lawn.chrisizworski.com generates a graded lawn plan from a single Michigan address. The tool combines soil, weather, and turfgrass context with a Claude-generated plan, then caches the result so identical addresses return instantly. Cornerstone articles, a city directory, and a curated shop layer support the core planning workflow.

Great Lakes Gazette

The Great Lakes Gazette is a Great Lakes maritime publication connecting vessel movement, lake conditions, and regional weather context. It gives the region's shipping and water information a place in the broader publishing work.

Freighter View Farms

Freighter View Farms is the seed-saving and Great Lakes gardening blog. The site focuses on heirloom vegetables, Zone 6a Michigan gardening, and the slow accumulation of a place-based seed library on the western shore of Saginaw Bay. Writing on the site is intentionally literary and grounded in a specific garden rather than generic gardening advice.
